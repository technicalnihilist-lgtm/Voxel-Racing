# Changelog

**Daily maintenance campaign — 1 to 20 November 2026, 10 updates per day.**
The UI is frozen: no entry below changes CSS, HUD, menus, layout or styling.
Every item is a bug fix, a performance optimisation or a fluidity improvement.

## 2026-10-31 — pre-campaign fixes
- Fixed a per-frame exception (`rp` was both the rain-drop array and the roll-rate spring) that aborted the render loop every frame.
- Cockpit camera sat *inside* the car body; it is now a hood cam just above the roof.
- Added lateral tyre grip + a grip-based yaw cap: the car tracks its nose instead of spinning out off-road (slip angle 23° → 7°).
- Replaced the teleporting off-road clamp with real guardrails; the rail now guides the car along instead of stopping it dead.
- Clamped suspension travel so a hard impact can no longer tilt the body through the floor.
- Wet asphalt re-tuned: dark and glossy instead of washed-out white.
- Countdown now runs on wall-clock time, so it no longer drags on slow devices.
- Default quality raised to High; adaptive resolution floored at 85% so the image never goes blurry.

## 2026-11-02

- Restarting a race rewound the skid-mark ring buffer (`skidI=0`) but never cleared the instance matrices, so every tyre mark laid in the previous race stayed on the road for the rest of the session — black streaks where you had not driven this time, and only the first few of them ever got overwritten. `newGame()` now resets all 260 skid instances to the zero matrix, so a restart starts on a clean road.
- Every wheel was built from five separate spoke meshes, so each car spent 20 draw calls on spokes alone and the eight cars on screen at High pushed 562 draw calls a frame. The five spokes are now merged into one geometry per wheel (same boxes, same angles, same triangle count), taking each car from 66 to 50 meshes and the High preset from 562 to 434 draw calls a frame - 128 fewer draw submissions every frame, with the geometry count and the image unchanged.
- Restarting a race left the car and the dash carrying over the previous race: the countdown never calls `step()`, so the speedometer and gear readout still showed the speed you crashed at, and the body-roll/pitch springs plus the steering angle still had the car sitting tilted on the line with its front wheels turned until GO. `newGame()` now zeroes that derived state (forward speed, slip, throttle, brake, steer angle, body sway and load transfer), so a restart begins from a settled car.
- The particle system walked all 600 slots, re-uploaded four attribute buffers (~19 KB) and drew the mesh every single frame, even when no particle was alive - and spray only exists in the wet, dust only off-road and sparks only on a hit, so most frames have none at all. - Leaving the game in a background tab left the engine droning: a hidden tab stops the render loop, so `audio()` never ran again and the engine, tyre and rain gains stayed frozen at whatever they held when you switched away, even though the race had already been paused. The visibility handler now mutes those three gains as well, and the first visible frame restores them from the physics state - same engine sound while you drive, no noise from a tab you are not looking at.

## 2026-11-03
- The race clock was running during the standing start: `tt` accumulated on `state==='play'||state==='count'`, so the HUD read 0:03.0 the instant the lights went out and every checkpoint message was inflated by the three-second wait, even though `step()` never runs and the car cannot move before GO. The clock now only accumulates in `play`, so the first frame of a race reads 0:00.0 and checkpoint splits are real race time.
- `spawn()` picked a lane and a distance for a traffic car but never wrote its transform - the only code that positioned traffic lived in the `play` branch of the loop, so from the first frame until GO all seven cars sat stacked on one point at the world origin (the road passes through that spot at z=1354) and then teleported onto the track the instant the lights went out. `spawn()` now places the mesh as well, so cars are already sitting on the road through the menu and the countdown and nothing pops in at GO.
- The Ultra preset's render scale was the device pixel ratio itself (`pr:Math.min(devicePixelRatio,2)`), while `resizeView()` already multiplies every preset by `min(devicePixelRatio,1.75)` - so the ratio was counted twice, and only on Ultra. On a 1x display Ultra rendered at exactly the same resolution as High (a no-op preset), and on a 2x display it jumped to 3.5x, i.e. 6.17 MP against High's 1.54 MP - four times the fill cost for one step up in the menu, enough to stall a phone. Ultra is now `pr:1.25`, a plain multiplier like Low/Med/High: 0.79 MP against High's 0.50 MP on a 1x screen, and 2.41 MP against 1.54 MP on a 2x screen. Same look, one preset step above High, and no longer a framerate cliff.
- Traffic cars' wheels turned at `v/.38` while the player's do `v/.6` - the same .41-radius wheel, scaled 1.5, so the same physical wheel rolling on the same road. Every traffic car's wheels therefore spun 58% faster than the car was moving (20 m/s looked like 8.4 rev/s instead of 5.2, near enough to the five-spoke spacing to judder and read as wheelspin when you pass one). The traffic loop now uses the same `/.6` divisor, so a traffic wheel turns exactly as far as its car travels; no geometry, material, speed or handling value changed.
- `rebuild()` lays out the road, rails, trees, buildings and rocks for all 150 segments every time the car crosses a 4 m boundary — 12 to 20 times a second at race speed — and each of those ~2,500 instance transforms was built by setting an `Object3D`'s position/rotation/scale, letting it convert the Euler to a quaternion and run `Matrix4.compose`, then copying the 16 floats out. Nothing in the scene is ever rotated about anything but Y, so `put()` now composes that matrix straight into the instance buffer. The matrices are bit-identical (checked element-for-element across six track positions and all four quality presets, and the rendered frames match), and `rebuild()` falls from ~0.70 ms to ~0.46 ms at High — a third off the heaviest thing the main thread does during a race.
- The scenery rocks were laid `8+h*10` metres from the centre line, so 45% of them landed on the paved shoulder *inside* the 11.4 m guardrail — well within reach of a car the rail clamps to 9.9 m — and unlike the trees, rocks have no collision test anywhere in the game, so a boulder sitting on the tarmac verge could be driven straight through. They now start at the 14 m the trees use, which puts the nearest rock edge at 13.5 m: clear of the car's 11.5 m reach and behind the rail. Verified against the live instance buffer over 49 rebuild windows and 1,039 placed rocks — none inside the rail (was 45%, nearest edge 7.58 m) — with the same rock count, sizes, colours, spread and placement rule as before, and no change to collision, handling or the road itself.
- Every traffic car built two volumetric headlight-cone meshes in `addBeams()`, but the only thing that ever raises their opacity is `updateSky()` writing `car.beamMat.uniforms.uOp` — the player's car. A traffic car's cones therefore sat at alpha 0 for the entire session and were still submitted every frame: with the seven traffic cars of the High preset that was 14 additive, `depthWrite:false` draw calls on every single frame, 14 of the frame's 358 draw calls, for geometry that provably cannot put a pixel on screen. Only the player's car builds them now (its beams still come on at night, uOp 0.5). Verified pixel-identical — zero differing pixels between the frame with the traffic cones and the frame without them, night and day — and a High scene with seven traffic cars falls from 358 to 344 draw calls and from 171,594 to 171,146 triangles.
